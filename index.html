I'm Suvankar Sen, a PhD student in Electrical Engineering at UNIST. I build meta-optical photodetectors: detectors with a metasurface on top that lets them see what an ordinary camera can't, from the full spectrum and polarization to light beyond the visible.

I work with Prof. Minho Choi in Ulsan, South Korea. Before that I spent four years at RMIT University in Melbourne, on phase-change materials whose optical properties can be switched on demand, and earlier on perovskites for solar cells and LEDs. I trained as an electrical engineer, then as a nanotechnologist.

Research

My PhD topic is the meta-optical photodetector. An ordinary camera throws away most of what light carries: it records brightness in three broad colors and forgets the fine spectrum, the polarization, and everything outside the visible. I want detectors that keep it.

The tool is the metasurface, a layer of nanostructures thinner than the wavelength of lightEach nanostructure acts like a tiny antenna. By choosing its shape and size, you set how it delays, filters, or rotates the light passing through it, pixel by pixel.. Placed on a detector, it can sort light by color and polarization, or trap it so a thin absorber catches more of it. Designing one by intuition gets hard fast, so I design them in reverse: state what the light should do, and let an optimizer find the structure that does itThis is inverse design. I simulate with rigorous coupled-wave analysis (RCWA) and pair it with gradient-based or gradient-free optimization..

01 · structure
Metasurfaces
Subwavelength structures that shape the phase, amplitude, and polarization of light in a single flat layer.
02 · method
Inverse design
Start from the response you want and let optimization discover the geometry, so the computer does the guessing.
03 · device
Meta-optical photodetectors
Detectors that read spectrum and polarization directly, or catch telecom light in a thinner absorber, thanks to the metasurface on top.

Before metasurfaces, I worked on optical and electronic materials: phase-change thin films (VO₂, Sb₂Se₃) whose optical constants switch with heat or light, lead-free perovskites for solar cells and LEDs, and plasma processing of nanomaterials. That hands-on fabrication background is what I bring to the computational side.
