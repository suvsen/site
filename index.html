Research

An ordinary camera throws away most of what light carries. It records brightness in three broad colors, and forgets the rest: the fine spectrum, the polarization, everything outside the visible. I'm interested in sensors that keep it.

The tool I use is the metasurface, a layer of nanostructures thinner than the wavelength of lightEach nanostructure acts like a tiny antenna. By choosing its shape and size, you set how it delays, filters, or rotates the light passing through it, pixel by pixel., which can sort light by color and polarization before it ever reaches the detector. Designing one by intuition gets hard fast, so I design them in reverse: state what the light should do, and let an optimizer find the structure that does itThis is inverse design. I simulate with rigorous coupled-wave analysis (RCWA) and pair it with gradient-based or gradient-free optimization..

01 · structure
Metasurfaces
Subwavelength structures that shape the phase, amplitude, and polarization of light in a single flat layer.
02 · method
Inverse design
Start from the response you want and let optimization discover the geometry, so the computer does the guessing.
03 · application
Hyperspectral & polarization imaging
Compact sensors that record the full spectrum and polarization of a scene instead of three color channels.

Before metasurfaces, I worked on optical and electronic materials: phase-change thin films (VO₂, Sb₂Se₃) whose optical constants switch with heat or light, lead-free perovskites for solar cells and LEDs, and plasma processing of nanomaterials. That hands-on fabrication background is what I bring to the computational side.
